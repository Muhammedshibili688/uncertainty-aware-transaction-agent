# Uncertainty-Aware Transaction Agent: v0 Specification

Version: v0.1
Status: FROZEN FOR FIRST EXPERIMENT
Freeze date: 2026-08-26

## 1. Purpose of v0.1

The agent observes information about one online transaction while the true
transaction state is unknown.

The agent must choose whether to:

- approve the transaction;
- obtain one additional verification result;
- send the transaction for human review;
- stop the transaction.

The purpose of v0.1 is to study whether explicitly representing uncertainty can
improve these decisions.

## 2. Unit of decision

One test case represents one online transaction.

The agent receives the transaction's initial evidence -> 
creates a belief about whether it is legitimate or fraudulent -> 
and selects an action.

The hidden ground-truth label is used only after the decision to evaluate the
agent. 
The agent cannot read the ground-truth label while making its decision.

## 3. Hidden states

### 3.1 LEGITIMATE

Definition:

A transaction is LEGITIMATE when the simulated ground truth states that it was
made or authorized by the expected customer and was not part of fraudulent
activity.

A legitimate transaction may still look unusual. It can involve a large
amount, a new device, an unusual location or several recent purchases.

### 3.2 FRAUDULENT

Definition:

A transaction is FRAUDULENT when the simulated ground truth states that it
resulted from unauthorized or deliberately fraudulent activity.

The fraudulent state exists at transaction time. A later chargeback, customer
report or analyst investigation may reveal the state, but it does not create
the state.

A fraudulent transaction may still look familiar or use a known device.

### 3.3 Hidden-state rules

- Every simulated transaction must have exactly one true hidden state.
- LEGITIMATE and FRAUDULENT cannot both be true for the same case.
- Fraud subtypes will be represented as different scenarios, not separate
  hidden states in v0.1.
- UNKNOWN is not a hidden state.
- UNKNOWN describes the agent's lack of knowledge, not the real transaction.

## 4. Initial evidence

Initial evidence is the information already available when the transaction
first reaches the agent. The agent does not need to contact the customer or
request another check to obtain this information.

For v0.1, I am using three initial evidence variables:

1. amount_deviation
2. device_location_context
3. recent_velocity

I selected these variables because they represent three different parts of a
transaction: the amount being spent, the context in which it is being made,
and the customer's recent activity.

These variables are deliberately simple. The purpose of v0.1 is to understand
how evidence changes the agent's belief, not to reproduce a complete
production fraud-detection system.

### 4.1 amount_deviation

Definition:

`amount_deviation` describes how unusual the current transaction amount is
when compared with the customer's stated previous spending behaviour.

Why it is included:

I included amount deviation because a transaction that is much larger than a
customer's normal purchases may deserve more attention. For example, a
customer who normally makes small purchases may suddenly attempt a much larger
transaction.

However, an unusual amount is only a warning sign. People sometimes make
legitimate large purchases, so amount deviation cannot be used by itself to
decide that a transaction is fraudulent.

Available values:

- NORMAL
- MODERATE
- HIGH
- UNKNOWN

Meaning of each value:

- NORMAL: The amount is within or reasonably close to the customer's stated
  normal spending behaviour.
- MODERATE: The amount is outside the customer's normal behaviour, but it is
  still reasonably plausible.
- HIGH: The amount is substantially different from the customer's stated
  normal behaviour.
- UNKNOWN: There is not enough comparable customer history to determine whether
  the amount is unusual.

Availability:

This evidence is available before the agent makes its first decision.

What it does not prove:

A HIGH amount deviation does not prove that a transaction is fraudulent. A
customer may be travelling, making an important purchase or buying something
that is not part of their usual spending pattern.

Similarly, a NORMAL amount does not prove that the transaction is legitimate.
An attacker may deliberately choose an amount that resembles the customer's
normal behaviour.

Missing information:

If the simulated case does not provide enough comparable customer history, the
value must be UNKNOWN.

UNKNOWN represents missing information. It must not be treated as NORMAL or as
evidence that the transaction is safe.

Simulation rule:

Each simulated case will describe the customer's usual spending behaviour and
the current transaction amount.

I will assign:

- NORMAL when the amount is within or reasonably close to the stated normal
  range;
- MODERATE when it is outside the normal range but still plausible;
- HIGH when it is substantially outside the stated normal range;
- UNKNOWN when no comparable history is provided.

The actual amount and the reason for assigning the category will be recorded
in the simulated case.

These categories are simulation assumptions. They are not presented as
universal banking or fraud-detection thresholds.

### 4.2 device_location_context

Definition:

`device_location_context` jointly describes whether the transaction comes from
a device previously associated with the customer and whether its location is
usual for that customer.

In a real system, device familiarity might be obtained from a device
fingerprint, a trusted-device identifier, previous logins or similar technical
information.

The v0.1 simulation does not create or process real device fingerprints. It uses
only the simplified result: whether the device appears known or new.

Why it is included:

I included device and location context because a new device or an unusual
location may indicate that someone other than the expected customer is using
the account.

However, both signals can also have legitimate explanations. A customer may
replace their phone, use another computer, travel, use a VPN or connect through
a mobile network that reports a different location.

For this reason, device and location information will change the agent's
belief, but neither one automatically proves fraud.

Available values:

- KNOWN_DEVICE_USUAL_LOCATION
- KNOWN_DEVICE_UNUSUAL_LOCATION
- NEW_DEVICE_USUAL_LOCATION
- NEW_DEVICE_UNUSUAL_LOCATION
- UNKNOWN

Meaning of each value:

- KNOWN_DEVICE_USUAL_LOCATION: The device has been seen before and the location
  is consistent with the customer's stated behaviour.
- KNOWN_DEVICE_UNUSUAL_LOCATION: The device is familiar, but the location is
  unusual.
- NEW_DEVICE_USUAL_LOCATION: The device has not been seen before, but the
  location is consistent with the customer's stated behaviour.
- NEW_DEVICE_UNUSUAL_LOCATION: Both the device and location are unusual for the
  customer.
- UNKNOWN: The device or location information required to create the category
  is unavailable.

Availability:

This evidence is available before the agent makes its first decision.

What it does not prove:

A new device does not prove that an account has been compromised. A customer
may have bought a new phone or used a different computer.

An unusual location also does not prove fraud. Legitimate explanations include
travel, roaming, VPN usage and inaccurate location estimation.

A known device and usual location do not guarantee legitimacy either. An
attacker may have access to the customer's device or account.

Correlation rule:

Device and location changes can be connected to the same real-world event. For
example, a customer travelling with a new phone may produce both a new-device
signal and an unusual-location signal.

To reduce obvious double-counting, v0.1 combines device and location into one
evidence variable. It will not multiply them as two independent confirmations
of fraud.

Simulation rule:

The written description of each simulated case will explicitly state:

- whether the device is known or new; and
- whether the location is usual or unusual.

The combination of those two statements will directly determine the category.

If either part cannot be determined from the case, the value will be UNKNOWN.

### 4.3 recent_velocity

Definition:

`recent_velocity` describes whether the number or frequency of recent
transaction attempts is unusual compared with the customer's stated normal
activity.

Why it is included:

I included recent velocity because fraud can involve several transactions or
attempts within a short period. Examples include card testing, repeated payment
attempts or quickly moving money before an account is blocked.

However, high velocity does not prove fraud. A legitimate customer may make
several purchases close together while shopping, travelling or paying multiple
bills.

Velocity therefore changes the fraud belief, but it cannot make the final
decision by itself.

Available values:

- NORMAL
- ELEVATED
- HIGH
- UNKNOWN

Meaning of each value:

- NORMAL: The recent activity is consistent with the customer's stated normal
  behaviour.
- ELEVATED: The recent activity is higher than normal, but it still has a
  plausible legitimate explanation.
- HIGH: The case describes rapid or repeated activity that is strongly unusual
  for the customer.
- UNKNOWN: Recent transaction information is unavailable or insufficient.

Availability:

This evidence is available before the agent makes its first decision.

What it does not prove:

HIGH velocity does not prove fraud because legitimate customers can make
several purchases close together.

NORMAL velocity does not prove legitimacy because a fraudulent transaction may
be a single carefully chosen attempt.

Missing information:

If the simulated case does not include sufficient recent transaction history,
the value must be UNKNOWN.

UNKNOWN must remain missing information. It must not be interpreted as NORMAL
activity.

Simulation rule:

Each simulated case will describe the customer's normal recent activity and
their current recent transaction attempts.

I will assign:

- NORMAL when the activity is consistent with the stated normal behaviour;
- ELEVATED when it is above normal but still reasonably plausible;
- HIGH when the case contains rapid or repeated attempts that are strongly
  unusual for that customer;
- UNKNOWN when there is not enough information to compare recent activity.

The actual number and timing of attempts will be recorded in the simulated
case so that the assigned category can be reviewed.

These categories are simulation assumptions and not production fraud
thresholds.

## 5. Additional evidence

Additional evidence is information that is not automatically available at the
first decision point. The agent must deliberately request it.

For v0.1, the agent can request only one type of additional evidence:
step-up verification.

I am limiting v0.1 to one additional check so that the first experiment remains
small, understandable and testable.

### 5.1 step_up_verification

Definition:

`step_up_verification` represents a challenge intended to obtain additional
information about whether the expected customer is involved in the
transaction.

Possible examples include:

- 3DS authentication;
- confirmation through a trusted banking application;
- direct customer confirmation.

The v0.1 simulation represents the result of this process. It does not connect
to a real authentication service or contact a real customer.

Allowed values:

- NOT_REQUESTED
- PASS
- FAIL
- INCONCLUSIVE
- UNAVAILABLE

Meaning of each value:

- NOT_REQUESTED: The agent has not requested step-up verification.
- PASS: The verification was completed successfully.
- FAIL: The verification failed or was rejected.
- INCONCLUSIVE: A result was received, but it did not resolve the uncertainty.
- UNAVAILABLE: The verification could not be performed.

Why it is included:

I included step-up verification because the initial evidence may leave the
agent unsure about the correct action.

Without an additional check, the agent might be forced to approve a risky
transaction, stop a legitimate one or send too many transactions to human
review.

Step-up verification provides an intermediate option. Its result may give the
agent enough information to make a safer automatic decision before involving a
human.

I selected this check instead of requesting arbitrary additional information
because it has a clear outcome that can realistically change the decision.

What it does not prove:

A PASS result supports the possibility that the expected customer is involved,
but it does not prove that the transaction is legitimate. A customer can be
deceived into approving a fraudulent payment, and authentication systems can
also be compromised.

A FAIL result supports the possibility of fraud, but it does not prove fraud.
A legitimate customer may fail because of a forgotten password, a technical
problem, an expired session or confusion during verification.

An INCONCLUSIVE result means the check did not resolve the uncertainty.

All outcomes are treated as evidence that updates the agent's belief rather
than as final proof.

Cost:

Step-up verification is not free. It may introduce:

- decision delay;
- customer inconvenience;
- payment abandonment;
- technical or operational cost.

The numerical cost will be defined later as a clearly labelled simulation
assumption.

Safety limit:

The v0.1 agent can request step-up verification at most once.

After receiving PASS, FAIL, INCONCLUSIVE or UNAVAILABLE, the agent must choose
APPROVE, HUMAN_REVIEW or STOP. It cannot continue requesting checks.

## 5.2 Evidence interpretation rules

- No individual evidence value proves that a transaction is legitimate or
  fraudulent.
- A known device does not guarantee legitimacy.
- A new device does not prove fraud.
- An unusual location does not prove fraud.
- A normal amount does not guarantee safety.
- An unusual amount does not prove fraud.
- High velocity may have legitimate explanations.
- Normal velocity does not guarantee legitimacy.
- Successful step-up verification supports legitimacy but does not prove it.
- Failed verification supports fraud but does not prove it.
- UNKNOWN represents missing information and must not be interpreted as safe
  evidence.
- Device and location are represented jointly to reduce obvious
  double-counting.
- Amount deviation and recent velocity may both depend on customer history.
  Treating them separately is a simplifying v0.1 assumption and will be recorded
  as a limitation.

## 6. Actions

The agent has four possible actions:

1. APPROVE
2. GET_MORE_EVIDENCE
3. HUMAN_REVIEW
4. STOP

These are operational decisions. They are not hidden states.

For example, sending a transaction to human review does not mean it is
fraudulent. It means the agent does not have enough information to make a safe
automatic decision.

### 6.1 APPROVE

Meaning:

Allow the transaction to continue automatically.

Terminal:

Yes. The agent does not make another decision after approval.

Consequence:

If the transaction is actually fraudulent, the approval becomes a fraudulent
approval and may create financial loss.

Explanation:

Automatic approval is useful when the available evidence supports legitimacy
strongly enough that another verification or human review is unlikely to
justify its cost.

Approval avoids unnecessary customer friction, payment delay and human-review
work.

The exact belief or cost condition required for approval will be defined later.
A transaction will not be approved merely because one signal looks normal.

### 6.2 GET_MORE_EVIDENCE

Meaning:

Request the single allowed step-up verification.

Terminal:

No. This action creates another decision point after the verification result
is received.

Eligibility:

- Verification has not already been requested.
- Verification is available.
- The transaction is still uncertain enough that another result may affect the
  final decision.

Consequence:

The agent receives PASS, FAIL, INCONCLUSIVE or UNAVAILABLE, updates its belief,
and then chooses a terminal action.

Explanation:

GET_MORE_EVIDENCE is separate from HUMAN_REVIEW because they use different
resources and serve different purposes.

Step-up verification is an automated attempt to resolve uncertainty using one
specific check. Human review transfers the decision to a person and consumes
analyst time.

A borderline transaction may be resolved by a quick verification without
requiring human review. Keeping these actions separate allows the experiment
to measure whether selective verification reduces unnecessary review workload.

The agent should not request verification automatically for every
transaction. It should be used only when the possible result may change the
decision.

### 6.3 HUMAN_REVIEW

Meaning:

Stop automatic decision-making and transfer the case to a human reviewer.

Terminal for the agent:

Yes. The human review process is outside the v0.1 agent.

Consequence:

The case consumes human-review time and may introduce decision delay and
operational cost.

Important rule:

HUMAN_REVIEW does not mean the transaction is fraudulent. It means the agent
does not have enough reliable information to make a safe automatic decision.

Explanation:

Some transactions may contain missing, conflicting or inconclusive evidence.
Automatically approving or stopping these cases may create more harm than
asking a human to examine them.

A human reviewer may also have access to context that is not included in the
small v0.1 model.

Human review is therefore a safety option, but it is not free. The experiment
will measure the review rate so that the agent cannot appear successful simply
by sending every difficult transaction to a person.

### 6.4 STOP

Meaning:

Prevent the transaction from continuing automatically.

Terminal:

Yes. The agent does not make another decision after stopping the transaction.

Consequence:

If the transaction is actually legitimate, stopping it becomes a legitimate
transaction stop or false decline.

Important rule:

STOP is an operational action. It does not change the hidden state and does not
prove that the transaction is fraudulent.

Explanation:

Stopping a genuinely fraudulent transaction can prevent financial loss and
protect the customer.

However, stopping a legitimate transaction can also cause harm. The customer
may be unable to complete an important payment, may lose trust in the service
or may stop using it.

For that reason, STOP should be used only when the estimated cost of allowing
the transaction is greater than the expected harm of stopping it. The exact
costs and decision thresholds will be defined later as simulation assumptions.

## 7. Decision sequence

The v0.1 agent follows this sequence:

1. Receive the initial evidence.
2. Begin with:
   - P(LEGITIMATE)
   - P(FRAUDULENT)
3. Update the belief using:
   - amount_deviation;
   - device_location_context;
   - recent_velocity.
4. Choose:
   - APPROVE;
   - GET_MORE_EVIDENCE;
   - HUMAN_REVIEW;
   - STOP.
5. If GET_MORE_EVIDENCE is selected:
   - request step-up verification;
   - observe its result;
   - update the belief;
   - select APPROVE, HUMAN_REVIEW or STOP.
6. Record the final action.
7. Reveal the simulated hidden state.
8. Evaluate whether the decision was correct and what it cost.

Belief rule:

P(LEGITIMATE) + P(FRAUDULENT) = 1

## 7.1 Policy parameters

The following values will be defined separately before running the experiment:

- prior fraud probability;
- evidence likelihoods;
- approval threshold;
- additional-evidence threshold or region;
- human-review threshold or region;
- stop threshold;
- fraudulent-approval cost;
- legitimate-stop cost;
- human-review cost;
- verification cost.

Any value not supported by comparable data will be clearly labelled as a
simulation assumption.

The v0.1 experiment will not describe its thresholds as universal fraud-industry
standards.

## 8. Feedback and evaluation

### Decision-time information

The agent may use:

- amount_deviation;
- device_location_context;
- recent_velocity;
- step_up_verification, if requested.

### Evaluation-only information

The agent cannot use the following while deciding:

- true hidden state;
- later confirmed fraud;
- eventual chargeback;
- final analyst determination.

These outcomes are revealed only after the simulated decision.

### Evaluation measures

The experiment will report:

- fraudulent approvals:
  FRAUDULENT transactions receiving APPROVE;

- legitimate stops:
  LEGITIMATE transactions receiving STOP;

- human-review rate:
  proportion receiving HUMAN_REVIEW;

- evidence-request rate:
  proportion receiving GET_MORE_EVIDENCE;

- total simulated decision cost;

- action-by-hidden-state results.

An unlabeled or delayed transaction will not automatically be counted as
LEGITIMATE.

## 9. Required research-informed scenarios

The v0.1 dataset must include at least:

- a legitimate traveller with a new device and unusual location;
- a legitimate customer making an unusually large purchase;
- a fraudulent transaction using a known device or usual location;
- a high-velocity legitimate shopping sequence;
- a card-testing or rapid-attempt fraud scenario;
- a transaction with PASS verification that is still fraudulent;
- a legitimate transaction with FAIL or INCONCLUSIVE verification;
- a transaction resembling historical behaviour that is nevertheless
  fraudulent;
- a case with missing evidence;
- a case where several apparently separate signals arise from the same
  underlying behavioural change.

## 10. Fixed v0.1 assumptions

- The hidden state is binary: LEGITIMATE or FRAUDULENT.
- The experiment uses simulated transactions.
- Evidence values are categorical.
- The true state is hidden from the agent while it decides.
- No single evidence value proves fraud or legitimacy.
- Device and location are represented jointly.
- Amount deviation and velocity are treated separately as a simplifying v0.1
  assumption.
- Missing evidence is represented as UNKNOWN.
- UNKNOWN is not treated as safe evidence.
- The agent may request only one additional verification.
- Human review remains a valid terminal action.
- Unlabelled or delayed outcomes are not automatically treated as legitimate.
- Probabilities, costs and thresholds without comparable sources will be
  labelled as simulation assumptions.

These assumptions keep the first experiment small enough to understand and
test. They are not claims about how a complete production fraud system should
work.

## 11. Out of scope

The following are outside v0.1:

- Production deployment
- Processing real financial transactions
- Using real customer personal data
- Automatically blocking actual customers
- An LLM generating fraud probabilities
- More than two hidden states
- Multiple rounds of evidence collection
- Real-time learning from customer outcomes
- Universal banking or fraud thresholds
- Modelling every reason a payment can fail
- Claiming that the simulation represents production accuracy

Explanation:

These are intentionally excluded because v0.1 is a small simulation
designed to test the agent's decisions.

A production fraud system would require real data, security controls, privacy
protections, monitoring, legal review, reliable authentication systems and
much more extensive validation.

Including the concerns listed above would make it harder to determine whether
the basic uncertainty-aware decision process works.
